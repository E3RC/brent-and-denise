How Much Does It Really Cost to Live Abroad?

Brent & Denise · August 20, 2026

We have tracked our monthly expenses for two years to build an honest comparison. Our budget in suburban Ohio runs about forty-eight hundred dollars a month: mortgage, two car payments, groceries, utilities, health insurance premiums, and the usual household costs. In Portugal, a two-bedroom apartment in a midsize city runs seven to nine hundred euros. Groceries for two average three hundred fifty euros. Private health insurance with good coverage runs about one hundred fifty euros per person per month. Our projected monthly budget there lands near twenty-two hundred euros, less than half of what we spend now.

Southeast Asia is cheaper still. In Chiang Mai, a modern furnished apartment costs around four hundred fifty dollars. Eating out at local restaurants runs two to three dollars a meal. A couple can live on under fifteen hundred dollars a month, including private insurance. The trade-off is distance from family and the need for reliable internet if we work remotely or stay connected back home.

Healthcare deserves its own paragraph. Our US plan costs twelve hundred dollars a month in premiums with a six-thousand-dollar deductible. Portugal's national health system is available to residents for a nominal fee, and private coverage for both of us runs about eighteen hundred dollars a year. In Thailand, top-tier international coverage that includes evacuation insurance runs roughly two thousand dollars annually. These are established systems serving millions of expatriates, not exotic arrangements.

Moving abroad is not for everyone. But if you are retired or close to it and watching your budget shrink every year, the numbers deserve a serious look. We built a detailed spreadsheet with a line item for every expense category and will share a downloadable version in a future post.
